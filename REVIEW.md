# Review list

Words, readings or choices that need a human check. Tick each one once checked.

## Stage 1 hiragana

- [ ] **る (ru)**: very few common beginner words *start* with る (the main one is るす "being away
      from home", which is abstract). Used はる haru "spring" instead, which has る at the end.
- [ ] **を (o/wo)**: を only appears as a grammar particle, never inside a normal word. Used the short
      phrase みずをのむ mizu o nomu "to drink water" as the sample.
- [ ] **ん (n)**: no Japanese word starts with ん. Used にほん Nihon "Japan" (also read にっぽん Nippon).
- [ ] **はし (hashi)**: means "chopsticks" (箸) here. The same kana also means "bridge" (橋); only the pitch
      differs (chopsticks: HA-shi, high then low).
- [ ] **お (o)**: おちゃ ocha. The お here is the polite prefix お (お茶). Still a natural, very common word.

## Stage 2 hiragana (voiced)

- [ ] **ぐ (gu)**: ぐあい guai "condition (how you feel)", as in ぐあいがわるい "I feel unwell".
      Correct, but a bit abstract for a beginner.
- [ ] **ぱ / ぴ (pa / pi)**: almost no everyday native words start with ぱ or ぴ (mostly sound words).
      Used かんぱい kanpai "cheers!" and えんぴつ enpitsu "pencil", which contain them.
- [ ] **ぺ (pe)**: ぺこぺこ pekopeko "starving" is a sound-symbolic word (おなかがぺこぺこ "I'm starving").
- [ ] **ぽ (po)**: さんぽ sanpo "a walk" contains ぽ rather than starting with it.

## Stage 3 hiragana (combinations)

Many combinations rarely start a common word, so some samples contain the sound instead.

- [ ] **にゃ**: こんにゃく konnyaku, a jelly-like food (common in oden). Mark may not know it.
- [ ] **にょ**: にょろにょろ nyoronyoro "wriggling (like a snake)", a sound-symbolic word. No common
      everyday word starts with にょ.
- [ ] **ひゅ**: ひゅうひゅう hyūhyū "whoosh (wind)", a sound-symbolic word.
- [ ] **ひょ**: ひょうしき hyōshiki "sign (road sign)". Correct, but not an early beginner word.
- [ ] **みゃ / りゃ**: みゃく myaku "pulse" and りゃく ryaku "abbreviation". Correct, but not common
      beginner words; few words start with these.
- [ ] **みゅ, びゅ, ぴゅ**: no native hiragana word uses these. The examples are katakana loanwords
      ミュージック "music", ビュッフェ "buffet", コンピューター "computer" (the matching katakana is highlighted).
- [ ] **ぴょ**: ぴょんぴょん pyonpyon "hopping", a sound-symbolic word.

## "Sounds like" hints

Filipino words were chosen so the highlighted letters make the same sound. Please check
that each Filipino word and meaning is right and natural to you.

- [ ] **English examples are approximate.** English vowels differ from Japanese: "car",
      "bar", "shark" have an r that Japanese doesn't; "go", "coat", "toe", "boat" glide
      ("oh-oo") where Japanese お is short and pure. The Filipino word is the better guide.
- [ ] **r row (ら り る れ ろ)**: no English word, because English r is a different sound.
      Filipino radyo, rito, ruta, relo, rosas.
- [ ] **ch / j sounds use Filipino spelling**: tsinelas (chi), tsaa (cha), tsokolate (cho),
      dyip (ji), dyaket (ja), dyus (ju), dyosa (jo). In Filipino, "ts" is usually said "ch"
      and "dy" is said "j"; some speakers say "ts" literally.
- [ ] **No Filipino word** for し shi, つ tsu, ふ fu, ゆ yu, ざ ず ぜ ぞ (z sounds), and several
      combinations; those cards show only the English word and/or the "built from" line.
- [ ] **ん (n)**: Filipino hi**n**di, English pe**n**. ん changes sound before some letters
      (like Filipino "ng" before k/g); the tip explains this.
- [ ] **Combinations with no word in either language** (ひゃ ひょ みょ りゃ りゅ りょ ぎゃ ぎゅ
      ぎょ びゃ びょ ぴょ): only the "built from" line, e.g. ひ (hi) + や (ya), said as one beat.
- [ ] **Less obvious Filipino picks**: pakyaw (kya, "buying in bulk"), kanya (nya, "his/hers"),
      niyog (nyo, said "nyog"), nipa (ni), hoy (ho), heto (he).
- [ ] **English picks to double-check**: Myanmar (mya), menu (nyu), huge (hyu), beauty (byu),
      Tokyo (kyo), cute (kyu), tsunami (tsu), bazaar (za).

## Example sentences (kana cards)

All 208 sentences were rewritten (2026-10-25) around travel scenes (convenience stores,
restaurants, airports, hotels, stations) and describing words, each with a word-by-word
breakdown, a pattern and a "try it with other words" example. A few to look at:

- [ ] **Word meanings in the breakdowns** are short and simplified for a beginner (e.g. は is
      shown as "topic: as for…", が as "subject marker"). Good enough to start; a textbook
      explains the full picture.
- [ ] **Some sample words are hard to use in travel**, so their sentences are a stretch:
      にょろにょろ (an eel wriggling), ひゅうひゅう (wind), ぴょんぴょん (hopping), ヒューズ (a fuse
      blowing), ヘリコプター (a helicopter night view), ゴルフ, ギター.
- [ ] **きつえん ゾーン** (ゾ card): 喫煙ゾーン is understood, but signs more often say 喫煙所 / 喫煙エリア.
- [ ] **ユーロも つかえますか** (ユ card): most shops in Japan take only yen; the sentence is about asking.
- [ ] **Facts in sentences**: Aomori apples, Hokkaido potatoes and snow, Kyoto temples, Tokyo
      having more people than Osaka, 「マクド」 for McDonald's in Osaka. All well known, but
      general statements.
- [ ] **Prices** (100-yen pen, 300-yen coin locker, 800-yen lunch, 200-yen bus) are examples,
      not real prices.

## Travel words and phrases

- [ ] **からい (karai)**: "spicy"; in some regions it also means "salty". Both noted on the card.
- [ ] **おおきに (ōkini)**: Osaka-dialect "thank you". Friendly and common in Osaka shops; in
      formal situations ありがとう ございます is safer.
- [ ] **だいじょうぶです** as "no thank you": very common, but it can be ambiguous; staff may
      ask again. Shaking your head or saying いいえ, けっこうです also works.
- [ ] **Place names in phrases**: おおさかじょう (Osaka Castle) and うめだ (Umeda) are examples;
      the notes say to swap in other places.
- [ ] **Kanji left out on purpose** for phrases normally written in kana (こんにちは, ありがとう,
      すみません, いただきます, ごちそうさまでした).
